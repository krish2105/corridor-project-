Finding 1 — the second survey day was not observed

Critical

12 May is derived from 11 May

Comparing every movement-by-class daily total across the two days gives $SERIES independent series. If both days were counted, their totals would differ in both directions in roughly equal measure.

$IDENT identical to the vehicle ($IDENTPCT%) $GREATER higher on day 2 $SMALLER lower on day 2

$IDENT of $SERIES series reproduce the previous day’s total exactly, while the 15-minute bins underneath them differ — values redistributed, total pinned. Of the series that do move, $GREATER rise and $SMALLER fall. Under independent counting that split is p ≈ 2×10−39.

In the two dominant classes, only 0.21% of 13,158 live bins fall on day two. Traffic does not rise at every approach of every junction in every quarter-hour.

Finding 2 — the survey omits the movement the scheme is built on

Critical

JDA is converting this road to U-turn operation. No U-turn was counted.

JDA has a scheme to make $ROAD signal-free, replacing junction signals with seven U-turn bays. The junctions it names for redesign — Bhrigu Path, Rajat Path, VT Road, Patel Marg, Vijay Path and B-2 Bypass — are the same six this survey counted. $NAMEMATCH of the survey’s own arm labels match those names exactly.

The survey recorded Left, Straight and Right. It contains no U-turn column anywhere. The scheme’s entire operating principle is converting turning movements into U-turns, and the traffic evidence base for it does not measure U-turns at all.

The drawing shows the demand is real. Along the $CKM km alignment, $UTURNS gaps in the median are wide enough for a vehicle to turn — $UTPERKM per km. $UTTYP are typical notified openings, $UTMOUTH are junction mouths, and $UTMARG are marginal, passable by a two-wheeler or auto but not a car.

There is a second-order effect that matters more than the missing column. At a median opening the U-turn competes with the right turn for the same gap in opposing traffic. So the right-turn volumes the survey does report understate their own effect on capacity. The gap is not just incomplete data; it biases the movement that is usually the binding constraint.

Finding 3 — PCU conversion understates demand

Material

Static factors applied to a composition-dependent standard

IRC:106 gives each class two factors — one for when it is under 5% of the stream, one for when it is over 10% — and requires interpolation between them. PCU is a function of composition. The survey used one constant per class, in all twelve workbooks.

Two-wheelers are $TWSHARE% of this corridor. They are carried at PCU 0.50, the value IRC:106 reserves for a class below 5%. The correct factor is 0.75.

Correcting only the four columns that map one-to-one to IRC:106 raises corridor PCU from $TOTSURV to $TOTCORR — +$UPLIFT%. That is a floor, not the answer.

The remaining columns cannot be corrected at all. $CARSHARE% of the stream sits in a single column reading “Car, Taxi, Tempo, Auto Rickshaw & Pick up”, which mixes a car (1.0) with an auto-rickshaw (up to 2.0) and a pickup (up to 2.0). Taking the widest defensible bound on those columns puts the true correction anywhere between +$BANDLO% and +$BANDHI%.

Both numbers should travel together. +$UPLIFT% is the floor — every step of it is citable to IRC:106 and it does not move under questioning. But it is a floor, and the true correction is larger: the defensible range runs to +$BANDHI%. Quoting the floor alone understates demand; quoting a midpoint invites an argument about an assumption the data cannot settle.

That 60-point spread is the real cost of the class scheme. It is not uncertainty in the traffic; it is uncertainty manufactured by recording half the stream in one box. Resolving it needs a re-count to a proper class scheme, which is a finding in its own right.

Finding 5 — arithmetic, and what it revealed

Correctable

$DISC stored totals disagree with their own components

$UNDER understate and $OVER overstate, so this is scattered formula damage rather than a bias. Net effect on the bin-level grand total: an understatement of $NETGT vehicles. All are listed with file, sheet and row; none was corrected silently.

The clearest case: on TMC-01, movement sheet V_1 stores Total Slow = 0 where its own five slow-vehicle columns sum to 58. The grand total inherits the error, and the three movements leaving arm 1 then fail to reconcile with the arm’s approach total by exactly 58 vehicles.

Chasing that gap produced a more consequential result. Reconciling the approach and total sheets against the movement sheets matches at $CELLS of $CELLS cells — exactly, at every bin, for every class. They are not corroborating measurements. They are formulas over the twelve movement sheets.

So the workbook holds one primary dataset per junction, presented as twenty-two sheets. Junction inflow-outflow balance is an identity and proves nothing about survey quality, and any statistic run across all sheets counts the same observation three times.

What the survey does support

The defects above are real and they change the numbers. They do not make the data worthless. One day of classified counts at six junctions, correctly re-derived, still answers the question the corridor study exists to answer.

Verified

Turn geometry is correct for left-hand traffic

India drives on the left, so a left turn is the near-side movement onto the next arm clockwise, and the right turn is the one that crosses opposing traffic. The clockwise mapping used throughout this pipeline was checked against the direction header that each of the 144 movement sheets states about itself. All 144 agree.

Through movements decide the corridor case

An elevated through-carriageway only helps traffic that is not turning. Across the six junctions, through movements are $THRU% of traffic (range $THRULO–$THRUHI%). The project methodology sets roughly 70% as the level at which an elevated through-carriageway is well founded. This corridor sits on that line, and the junction-by-junction spread is where the argument actually lives.

Corridor constraints

The counts say what uses the corridor. The survey drawing says what is physically in the way — and it was sitting unconverted in the project folder. Read directly, it carries 1,041,959 entities across 44 layers, including a full utility survey.

Feasible

An elevated structure has room, and the argument is about five pinch points

Walking the $CKM km alignment at 25 m stations and counting what falls inside an 8 m pier footprint: $CFREE of $CSTN stations ($CFREEPCT%) carry no hard constraint — no building, temple, railway or gas main. The longest uninterrupted runs are $CRUN1 m and $CRUN2 m, ample for any pier spacing.

Reporting a single blended score would have been misleading, and nearly was. Weighted equally, 2,300 lamp posts outrank a building and the corridor reads as 74% blocked. Lamp posts get relocated as a matter of routine; a temple does not. Separating the two inverts the conclusion.

What remains unknown

  • U-turn demand. Never counted. Unmeasured, not zero — and U-turns concentrate at median openings, where they cost the most capacity.
  • E-rickshaws. No column anywhere holds them. A growing share of Jaipur traffic is simply absent from the count.
  • Half the PCU correction. Locked behind the composite class columns. Only re-counting to a proper class scheme resolves it.
  • Junction coordinates. The workbooks carry no georeference at all. Recovering the corridor order from flow continuity alone gives $ORDER, but at a $MARGIN% margin over the runner-up — that is noise, not a result. Mid-block frontage access breaks continuity too badly. Map pins are needed.
  • A representative week. 11 May 2026 is a Monday, which the methodology excludes; May is pre-monsoon peak heat against a recommended October–November window; and no weekend day was surveyed.
  • Why PHF runs 0.93–0.98. Typical urban Indian arterials sit at 0.85–0.92. Flow this uniform across the peak hour suggests a smoothed series, and peak-15 design values taken from it will be understated.
